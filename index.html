Privacy Policy

OPlayer

Last updated: October 24, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how OPlayer (“we,” “us,” or “the App”) collects, uses, and protects your information when you use our Android application for video and music playback.

1. Information We Collect

1.1 Information Collected by the App Itself

OPlayer is a local video player, music player, and media library browser. It does not require you to register or sign in, and it does not upload your video files, audio files, or full media library to our own servers. Specifically:

  • We do not require collection of your name, postal address, government-issued identification number, or similar information;
  • We do not actively read or upload device identifiers that can directly identify an individual, such as IMEI, device serial number, ANDROID_ID, Advertising ID (GAID), or Wi-Fi MAC address, for our own server-side storage;
  • Theme, language, video and music player settings, decoder options, equalizer and sound options, gesture preferences, library preferences, and other app settings are stored locally on your device (DataStore / SharedPreferences);
  • Playback history, resume positions, favorites, playlists (video and music), search history, queue state, lyrics edits you save, and network URL history you use in the app are stored locally on your device (Room database and DataStore);
  • The app scans local videos and audio files through Android MediaStore, the system file picker, and related system APIs, reads file paths, file names, duration, size, album/artist/genre tags, cover art, and similar metadata, and displays them in the library UI. This media information is processed on your device and is not uploaded to our servers to provide core playback features;
  • When you play a network video stream, the app connects to the URL you enter or open. That connection is between your device and the remote server or service you choose. We do not operate that remote content server;
  • When you use optional music features such as editing tags, editing lyrics, exporting a playlist, or setting a song as ringtone/alarm, processing occurs on your device and only when you actively use those features;
  • When you take a screenshot during video playback, the image is saved locally (for example, under Pictures/OPlayer on your device) only when you actively use that feature;
  • When the app crashes, a local crash screen may show technical logs on your device. You may choose to copy or share those logs through the system share sheet. Separately, crash diagnostics may be sent to Firebase Crashlytics as described in Section 1.2.

[Important] About your videos, music, and media files: OPlayer reads and plays video and audio files that you already have on your device or that you open through a link. Video and audio content itself is not transmitted to our servers. Media management actions such as rename, delete, share, edit tags, or open with another app occur only when you actively initiate them, using Android system APIs on your device.

Opening media from other apps: If you open a video, audio file, or streaming link from another app (for example, through “Open with” or a share intent), OPlayer receives the URI or link needed to start playback. We do not assume separate privacy obligations for how the app that shared that content handled it.

Picture-in-picture and background playback: If enabled in settings and supported by your device, video or music playback may continue in a floating window or in the background through a foreground media playback service (and related notifications where required by Android). This processing stays on your device and is not uploaded to our own servers.

1.2 Information Collected by Third-Party Services

The app uses the following third-party services, which may collect certain information:

Firebase Analytics

We use Firebase Analytics to understand how users interact with the app and to improve the experience. Firebase Analytics may collect:

  • App usage events: screen or feature usage, flow statistics, and similar data;
  • Device information: device model, Android version, language settings, screen-related parameters, and similar data;
  • Performance and stability metrics: launch and runtime statistics (as actually provided by Google).

Important: Firebase Analytics is designed for product and aggregate analysis. It is not used to upload your video files, audio files, or full local media library. For more information, see Google’s official documentation: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

Firebase Crashlytics

We use Firebase Crashlytics to automatically detect and report app crashes and errors so we can fix issues and improve stability. Crashlytics may collect:

  • Device information: device model, Android version, manufacturer, and similar data;
  • App state: information related to app operation at the time of a crash;
  • Error information: stack traces, error messages, crash logs, and other technical diagnostic information.

Important: Crashlytics is primarily used for technical diagnostics and app improvement. Crash reports may include file paths or technical context visible in a stack trace, but are not intended to include the content of your video or audio files. For more information, see Google’s official documentation: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

Firebase-related data is handled in accordance with Google’s Privacy Policy. For more information, see: https://policies.google.com/privacy

General note (not directly identifying individuals): Data collected through Firebase is generally processed in aggregated or de-identified form and is not used to directly identify a specific individual, and is not used to read or upload the video or audio files in your media library.

Important: We do not provide a user account system and do not operate our own servers for storing users’ personal data or media content. Data processed by Firebase is subject to Google’s data retention and security practices; retention periods follow Google’s rules.

2. Permissions Requested by the App

The permissions below are based on the Release merged manifest for OPlayer. The actual permissions shown on your device may differ by Android version; please refer to App info in your system settings.

2.1 Permissions Declared by This App

The following permissions are declared in our app manifest for core functionality:

Permission Purpose
android.permission.READ_MEDIA_VIDEO On Android 13 and above, read video files on your device so the app can scan your local video library, display folders and videos, and play local video files.
android.permission.READ_MEDIA_AUDIO On Android 13 and above, read audio files on your device so the app can scan your local music library, display songs/albums/artists/playlists, and play local music.
android.permission.READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE
(maxSdkVersion 32)		 On Android 12 and below, read video and audio files from shared storage so the app can scan and play local media.
android.permission.WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE
(maxSdkVersion 32)		 On older Android versions, save playback screenshots to your device and support certain local file operations (such as rename, delete, or tag edits) when you actively use those features.
android.permission.INTERNET Play network video streams from URLs you enter or open (for example HTTP, HTTPS, HLS, DASH, RTSP, RTMP, or similar protocols supported by the player), support optional local network casting features where available, and transmit Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics data as described above.
android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE Run a foreground service while video or music playback continues in the background, where supported by your settings and device.
android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK Identify the foreground service type as media playback on supported Android versions.
android.permission.POST_NOTIFICATIONS On Android 13 and above, show media playback and related notifications (for example, music controls) when required by the system.
android.permission.WRITE_SETTINGS Used only when you actively choose to set a song as the device ringtone or alarm, and only after you grant this optional system setting permission.

[Storage note] OPlayer needs access to video and audio files on your device to scan and play local media. On Android 10, the app may use legacy storage access behavior where supported by the system so that local file paths remain accessible for scanning and playback. The app does not request camera, microphone, precise location, contacts, or phone state permissions for core features. The app also does not request READ_MEDIA_IMAGES for core features; if a system dialog mentions “photos and videos,” that wording may reflect Android’s permission group label when video access is requested.

[Note] You can manage or revoke permissions in system settings. Revoking video or audio access will prevent the app from scanning or playing the corresponding local media. Revoking notification permission may limit media control notifications. Revoking network access will prevent network streaming and may affect Firebase diagnostics.

3. System Sharing, Opening Methods, and Other Services

When you use features such as Share, Open with another app, or Share crash logs, the app uses Android system intents (for example ACTION_SEND or FileProvider) so you can choose the destination app. This flow involves the Android system and the app you choose. We only pass the content you choose to share when you actively initiate sharing, and we do not assume separate privacy obligations for how third-party receiving apps handle that data.

When you enter or open a network video URL, playback data is exchanged between your device and the server or service at that URL. Network URL history is stored locally on your device for your convenience and is not uploaded to our own servers.

The app does not provide online social feeds, cloud media hosting, or user account services. If a future version adds new online features, we will update this policy and adjust permission and data security disclosures as needed.

4. How We Use Information

We use relevant information only for the following purposes:

  • Scanning, browsing, searching, and playing local video and audio files on your device;
  • Organizing music by songs, albums, artists, genres, folders, and playlists, and supporting features such as shuffle, queue, equalizer, sleep timer, lyrics display/editing, and optional ringtone settings when you use them;
  • Playing network video streams from URLs you provide or open;
  • Managing video playback features such as subtitles, audio tracks, playback speed, playlists, favorites, resume position, picture-in-picture, and optional background playback;
  • Saving screenshots locally when you actively choose to capture a frame during video playback;
  • Performing local media management actions such as rename, delete, share, or tag edits when you request them;
  • Improving app performance and stability, and identifying and fixing crashes and errors;
  • Understanding aggregated usage to optimize the experience and user flows.

5. Data Sharing and Disclosure

We do not sell, trade, or rent your personal information for unrelated marketing. Data processing or disclosure may occur only in the following circumstances:

  • Firebase services: Technical and statistical data collected or processed by Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics is handled by Google in accordance with its Privacy Policy;
  • Network playback: When you play a remote URL, your device communicates with the server or service you selected;
  • Your active sharing: Apps you choose through the system share sheet or “Open with” flow;
  • Legal requirements: Where required by applicable law, or in response to lawful requests from courts or other competent authorities, we may disclose necessary information as required by law.

6. Data Security

We take your information security seriously. Local media browsing, playback, and most preferences are processed on your device. When analytics and crash diagnostics are transmitted through Firebase, industry-common transmission and protection measures are typically used (such as HTTPS, as actually implemented by Google). We do not use your video or music library for sale or for purposes inconsistent with this policy. We do not transmit or store your video or audio files on our servers.

Android backup: The app may participate in Android backup/restore mechanisms according to system and manifest settings. Backup behavior depends on your device, Android version, and Google account settings. Locally stored playback history, preferences, and library metadata may be included in device backup if enabled by the system.
